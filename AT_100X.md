# At 100x

Assumption: 500 active projects, 60 people, 12 clients, each with its own
definition of "over-allocated".

## What breaks first — ingestion

- Napkin math: 500 projects → ~5,200 tasks → ~260 ClickUp pages, sequential.
  At ~0.5s expected cost per request (latency + retries), that's **~136s**. The
  run budget is 120s: **the agent dies by its own timeout.**
- The bottleneck isn't the 500 projects (bulk endpoints stay few requests) —
  it's the ClickUp pages.
- **Fix:** concurrent fetch with a concurrency limit, page as the retry unit (not
  the whole collection). Note: the stub uses a blocking `time.sleep()`, so
  concurrency yields little there; against real ClickUp, it would.

## What breaks second — the channel

- 500 projects, even at 10% risk, is ~50 items per message. Nobody reads it; the
  channel gets muted and the agent dies by noise, not by a bug.
- **Fix:** the top-5 by severity + "and N more" is already there. At this scale a
  persistent view (dashboard) is also needed, complementing the alerts.

## What breaks third — reconciliation

- With 60 people, more ambiguous name pairs per run; matching becomes the
  dominant cost if recomputed in full each time.
- **Fix:** a persistent identity table. Resolved once and stored; only new or
  changed people are evaluated. Builds on the human confirmations already stored.

## The definition stops being one

- 12 clients with 12 definitions of over-allocated. Today the threshold is in
  `config`.
- **Fix:** per-client rules in configuration (file or table), one engine with
  variable parameters. It's the reason the window is already configurable.

## Order of attack

1. **Concurrent ClickUp pagination** — the only thing that already breaks today
   at scale.
2. **Cut the message** — cheap; without it adoption dies even if everything works.
3. **Persistent identity table** — cuts the reconciliation cost.
4. **Per-client rules** — the most expensive and least urgent: it only hurts with
   the third client that has its own criterion.

Ordering criterion: first what's already broken, then what kills adoption, then
cost, and last flexibility.
